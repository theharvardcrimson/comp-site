Storytelling. Style. Impactful investigations.

Fifteen Minutes, The Crimson's weekly magazine, is Harvard's home for narrative
journalism. Since 1976, we have chronicled the people, politics, and phenomena
that shape our campus and city.

We publish across seven weekly content categories: The Scoop (in-depth
reporting), Conversations (personality profiles), Retrospection (historical
accounts), Introspection (personal essays), Around Town (scene reports), Levity
(satire), and Inquiry (cultural criticism). What unites our coverage is our
commitment to storytelling and style. We craft vivid stories about the characters
and cultural forces driving the news. We do so with wit, verve, and precision.

Our mission is made clearest by our cover stories. Scrutinies, as we like to call
them, are long-form deep dives into a variety of subjects — [from the Cambridge government's approach to reparations](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2024/10/12/cambridge-reparations-scrut/),
[to campus dialogue efforts in the wake of October 7th](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2024/11/22/harvard-tarek-masoud-israel-palestine-dialogue/),
[to student influencers leveraging the Harvard brand](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2024/9/29/harvard-influencer-scrut/).
Scrutinies consistently rank among the most-read articles on The Crimson's
website, and they are regularly honored at the national level by the Associated
College Press Awards and the Society for Professional Journalists.

At FM, we lend critical eyes and surprising angles to timely news threads,
publishing stories that [reach national audiences](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2002/11/21/the-secret-court-of-1920-at/)
and [create change on campus](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2022/4/7/adams-fireplace/).
We cultivate our writers' unique voices, refining their ideas through thoughtful,
rigorous editing. We want you to join us.
